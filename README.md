# xlcalc-sheet

Vue 3 sheet component for [xlcalc](https://github.com/drakosha/xlcalc) workbooks: a windowed grid with the book's styles, formulas and editing, which talks to its data only through a `SheetSource` (`src/source.ts`). MIT.

```vue
<script setup lang="ts">
import { SheetView, memorySource, ruMessages } from 'xlcalc-sheet'
import 'xlcalc-sheet/style.css'

const source = memorySource({ sheets: [{ name: 'Sheet1', cells: { A1: 'Hello', B1: 42 } }] })
</script>

<template>
  <SheetView :source="source" :messages="ruMessages" storage-key="my-book" style="height: 480px" @state="console.log($event)" />
</template>
```

The runtime depends only on `vue`: xlcalc's engine-free subpaths `xlcalc/a1` and `xlcalc/text` (addresses, the references of formula text, a paste's rectangle) are bundled in, and a book in ru-RU loads ru-RU's formula text as a chunk of its own. Colours and fonts are `--xs-*` CSS variables. `npm run dev:example` serves the example with every kind of formatting, `npm run screens` takes its screenshots, `docs/host-parity.md` maps the component onto the the app's grid.

## Keys and the source

Editing takes Excel's keys: typing over a cell, F2 or a double click opens the field, Enter, Tab and their Shift commit and move, Esc cancels, Alt+Enter breaks the line, Delete and Backspace clear the selection. Without `edit` the sheet is read-only. The source's optional methods turn on the rest:

- Control+Return (Cmd+Return on a Mac), in the cell's field or the formula bar, enters the text into every cell of a selection through `fill(sheet, range, text, active)`, as Excel enters it from the active cell; the selection stays, and the text goes even when it did not change. Without `fill`, or on one cell, the text goes to the active cell through `edit` and the cursor does not move.
- A paste on the grid goes to `paste(sheet, cell, text)` in one call at the selection's top-left cell, and the source reads the text as Excel pastes it. Without `paste` the component lays the text out as Excel pastes it (xlcalc's `parseClipboard`: a row at each line break, a field at each tab, a line break at the end one more row of empty fields) and sends the fields to `edit`; a rectangle that would leave the sheet is refused. A paste of the sheet's own copy goes through `structure` instead (below).
- Control+Z and Control+Y (Cmd on a Mac; Control+Shift+Z redoes too; any keyboard layout) on the grid call `undo()` and `redo()`, one call at a time. In the field they undo the typing. Without the methods there is no undo.
- A successful outcome's `select` is where the selection goes after a paste, a fill, an undo or an edit; on another sheet the component goes there as the address box does. A selection moved while the call was out stays where it was moved.
- A window's `editText` is the text a cell opens with, and the formula bar shows the same text, as Excel's does; a cell left out of it opens empty, and a `CellUpdate` with `editText` changes it. Without it the component writes the text from the formula and the value.
- A column whose width is `null` in a window is 8.83203125, xlcalc's default (Excel for Mac, Calibri 11), which is 62 px.
- A window's `appearance` is what conditional formats give its cells, as xlcalc computes it (`ConditionalAppearance`), and the grid draws it as Excel does: a rule's style over the cell's own (font colour, bold, italic, underline, strike, fill, borders; a fill hides grid lines as the cell's own does), a colour scale's colour as a fill, a data bar from its `start` to its `end` (from the right with `rightToLeft`; a gradient from the bar's colour at its axis toward white, or solid; a border; the axis dashed across the cell), an icon of any of the 20 sets at the cell's left before its text, and with `hideValue` the bar or the icon alone (an icon alone stands by the cell's own alignment, at the left for General). A cell's `appearance` takes the place of its `bars` entry; `bars` alone is drawn as a gradient bar from the cell's left edge. The text and colour a rule's number format gives are the window's `text` and `colors`.
- `subscribe(listener)` tells the sheet of changes made elsewhere. `changed` patches the loaded windows; `reload` reads the sheets and windows again and applies a display filter again; `repaint` with ranges reads again only the windows that meet them on the sheet shown, in place: each stays drawn until its answer comes, and the sizes, the scroll and the display filter's rows stay as they are (a row an edit took out of the filter stays until the filter is applied again, as Excel keeps it until Reapply). A second `repaint` aborts the request of the first, and a `changed` that comes while a window is read goes over its answer.
- Control+B, Control+I and Control+U (Cmd on a Mac, any keyboard layout) on the grid toggle bold, italic and underline over the selection by the active cell's look, through `format`, as the toolbar's buttons do. Without `format` the browser keeps the keys.
- A click on a column letter or a row number selects the whole line to Excel's last row or column, so the `state` event and the address box give `B:D`, `2:5` or `1:1048576` for the whole sheet, and formatting goes on the lines.

## Rows, columns and sheets

A right click on a row number or a column letter, or a finger held on it for half a second, opens Excel's menu for the selected lines (a line outside a selection of whole lines is selected first): Insert and Delete as many lines as are selected through `structure(op)`, Row Height or Column Width in Excel's units (points; characters of the default font, the width Excel's box shows) with Excel's limits, Hide, and Unhide on a selection over hidden lines, through `layout(change)`; a size of 0 hides the lines. The same on a tab makes that sheet the active one and offers Insert (before it, under Excel's next name: `Sheet4`, `Лист4` with `ruMessages`), Delete (asked first when the sheet has data), Rename (in place on the tab, a double click too), Move Left and Move Right among the shown tabs, Hide, and Unhide with the list of hidden sheets. A refusal shows the source's message (`./local` gives the library's, in the book's locale), and after a change the sheet reads its sheets and windows again unless the source sent `reload`. The sheet shown after is Excel's: a sheet inserted or unhidden, and for one deleted or hidden the next shown sheet, or the one before it at the end. Without `structure` or `layout` their items are gone, without both the browser's own menu stays, and a read-only sheet has none.

A sheet whose `SheetInfo.outline` has groups gets Excel's outline bars: the rows' left of the row numbers, the columns' above the letters, a slot for each level and one more. Each expanded group has a thin bracket along its lines with a tick at the end away from its summary line, and a − button on the summary line (below the group, or above it without `summaryBelow`; right of columns, or left without `summaryRight`); a collapsed group has only its +. The level buttons 1 to N+1 sit in the corner: the rows' across their bar at the letters' height, the columns' down their bar over the numbers. A group's button goes to `outline({ op: 'collapse' | 'expand', sheet, rows: '3:6', level })` (`columns: 'B:D'` for columns), a level button to `outline({ op: 'showLevel', sheet, axis, level })`, and the sheet reads itself again after them unless the source sent `reload`. Without `outline`, or on a read-only sheet, the bars are drawn and their buttons do not press. Under a display filter the rows' bar is left out, as its rows are not the sheet's in order. On a finger the bars' slots are wider and a tap on a bar presses the nearest button within 22 px, a target of 44 px around a button drawn at Excel's size. The menu on numbers and letters has Group and Ungroup over the selected lines, as do Excel's keys, Shift+Alt+Right and Left (Cmd+Shift+K and J on a Mac); over a selection of cells a small box asks rows or columns, rows first, as Excel's does. What Excel does not do is said and not sent: a Group past the seventh level, an Ungroup of lines in no group. The bars' colours and the buttons' size are `--xs-outline-*` variables.

The tabs show with one sheet too and end with "+", which inserts a sheet at the end and shows it (with `structure`); hidden sheets are not on them. A double click on a column's edge fits the column to what it shows through `layout({ columns, fit: true })`, every selected whole column when it is one of them, as Excel does; without `layout` it brings back the book's width. Two properties of `SheetView` give back the the app's ways: `hide-lone-tab` (no tabs for a book of one sheet) and `edge-restores-width` (the double click brings back the book's width).

## Copy, cut and paste; the fill handle

Control+C (Cmd+C on a Mac) puts the selection on the clipboard as text, tab-separated as Excel's, and marks the range with Excel's moving dashed border; Control+X does the same as a cut. With `structure` on an editable sheet, Control+V of that same text, or a paste whose clipboard the browser does not give, pastes the range itself: a copy through `structure({ op: 'copyRange', from, to })`, as tiles into a selection whose size the copy divides and else once from the selection's top-left cell, and a cut through `moveRange` to the selection's top-left cell, on another sheet of the book too. The source does the operation, so formulas move as Excel moves them (`./local` hands it to xlcalc's `editStructure`). The pasted range is selected, and a refusal shows the source's message. After a paste a copy's border stays until Esc, a cell's field opened, a change of cells (an edit, a fill, Delete, an undo) or another copy; a cut's goes once it is pasted. Once the border is gone, its text is not pasted again, as Excel has nothing to paste then. Any other text is pasted as before (`paste`, else `edit`), and formatting and lines leave the border on. Without `structure` a copy is pasted as its text. Under the display filter a copy is the text of the shown rows, unmarked, and there is no cut.

A right click on a cell, or a finger held on it, opens Excel's menu for cells over the selection (a cell outside it is selected first): Cut, Copy and Paste, 44 px high on a finger, then the commands of the next section the source allows. They go through the browser's clipboard API. Paste reads the clipboard, which the browser may ask the person to allow; where it will not, Paste pastes only the sheet's own copy and otherwise says to use Control+V. A read-only sheet's menu has Copy and Trace Precedents alone.

Excel's fill handle is the small square at the selection's bottom-right corner, on an editable sheet with `structure`, but not under the display filter, over whole columns or rows, or while a field is open. Dragged down, up, right or left, its frame grows along the axis the pointer went furthest, by whole tiles of the selection, and scrolls the grid at its edge; on release the selection is copied over the cells added through `copyRange`, one step of undo, and both are selected. A double click on it fills down as far as the data of the column left of the selection goes, else of the column right of it. On a finger its target is 44 px around a square drawn small, and a tap on it picks the cell under it. Excel's handle also extends series (1, 2 → 3, 4; dates by day; Item1 → Item2) and fills a part of the selection past its last whole tile; `copyRange` copies whole tiles alone, so the frame grows by the selection's size and a series is copied as it is, as Fill Down copies it. The border's and the frame's colours are `--xs-copy-line` and `--xs-fill-frame`.

## Reapply, Freeze Panes and the precedents

Under a display filter the cells' menu has Excel's Reapply, as do Control+Alt+L (on a Mac too, where Cmd+Option+L is the browser's) and a button on the filter's line at the bottom: the conditions and the sort are applied again to the data as they are now, through `visibleRows`, so a row an edit took out of them goes and one it brought in comes. Until then such a row stays, as in Excel. The scroll stays, and so does the active cell unless its row went, when the row now at its place takes it.

With the source's `freeze(sheet, panes)` on an editable sheet the menu has Excel's Freeze Panes, Freeze Top Row and Freeze First Column, and on a sheet the book freezes Unfreeze Panes takes the first one's place. Freeze Panes keeps the rows above and the columns left of the active cell in view, so a selection of whole columns freezes the columns left of it and one of whole rows the rows above it; from A1 it freezes the rows and columns above and left of the frame's middle, as Excel splits the window there. Each is the source's step of undo, and the sheet reads itself again after it unless the source sent `reload`. The lock on the formula bar stays a view over the book's panes: it hides them or shows them again in this browser and leaves the book alone. A freeze made from the menu shows whatever the lock said before and however much of the frame the panes take, and an unfreeze forgets the lock's choice. Without `freeze` there are no such items, and the lock works as before.

With the source's `explain(sheet, cell)` the menu has Trace Precedents. Where Excel draws arrows, the sheet opens a panel right of the grid (below it on a phone) with the source's tree of the active cell's precedents. The panel follows the active cell while it is open: it asks again 150 ms after the cell stops moving or its value changes, and when the source tells of changes; an answer that comes after a newer request is dropped, as `explain` takes no signal. A line is a node and two spaces of indent are a level, as xlcalc's `explain` writes its text (`'Q 4'!A2 = 60  ← Data!A4*2`). The references in it to cells, columns or rows of one sheet are links, a reference without a sheet is on its line's node's sheet, and a click goes there as a reference in the formula bar does; the panel then explains the cell it went to. The cross and Esc close it. `./local` gives the session's `explain` in the book's locale, and `memorySource` writes a tree from the formulas as given, in xlcalc's English words. The panel's width, and its height on a phone, are `--xs-explain-w` (340 px) and `--xs-explain-h` (45 %).

## Find and Replace

Control+F, or the magnifier on the formula bar, opens Excel's Find under the formula bar when the source has `find(req, signal)`. The sheet asks it with a `FindRequest`: the query with Excel's wildcards (`*` any characters, `?` one, `~` before either of them or itself for that character) and the options of Excel's dialog under the bar's Options button: Match case, Match entire cell contents, Look in formulas (the text a cell opens with, Excel's default) or values (the text it shows), and Search by rows or by columns. A selection of more than one cell when the bar opens, or one selected while it is open, is where it looks (`range`), shown on the bar until the person drops it; under a display filter only its visible rows count. Enter and Shift+Enter, or the arrows, go to the next and the previous hit from the active cell in the order asked, round past the end, as Find Next does, and a new query or option goes to its first hit after the active cell. Hits in the frame are tinted and the active one pulses.

Control+H (on a Mac too, as in Excel for Mac) opens the bar with its replace row (its toggle sits at the bar's left), when the sheet is editable and the source has `replace(req, by)`. Replace replaces the active cell when it is a hit and goes on to the next, elsewhere it only finds, as Excel's does; Replace All replaces every hit in one step of undo. Both look in formulas whatever the bar is set to, as Excel's Replace does, and their outcome goes as an edit's: its changes are drawn, a refusal shows in the sheet's message line. The bar then says how many cells changed and counts the hits again.

## Data validation

A source with `validation(sheet, cell, signal)` gives what Excel shows on a cell under a rule of data validation (`SheetValidation`): the items of a list rule's dropdown as their text, in order (empty when the list's formula gives none), and the input message, its title and text; null when no rule is over the cell. An edit or a Control+Return whose value a rule does not take is refused with `alert` (`SheetValidationAlert`): the rule's style, `stop`, `warning` or `information`, its title (none for Excel's "Microsoft Excel"), its message and the cell. After a warning's Yes or an information's OK the component sends the same call again with `{ accept: true }`, and the source writes the value without asking the rule again. A paste is not checked, as Excel does not check one. `memorySource` takes a sheet's `validations` (`MemoryValidation`: a list or whole numbers within bounds, an input message, the alert's style, title and message), enough for an example or a test; `./local` takes the book's rules (below).

The parts that show it are Excel's. `useValidation` (`src/core/validation.ts`) asks the source once the active cell stays for 60 ms, aborts the request of a cell left and drops its answer, and keeps the list's state: Alt+Down opens the list at the item the cell shows, its items asked again as Excel computes them on opening; the arrows, Page Up and Page Down (eight items, as many as the list shows), Home and End move; Enter or a click enters the item into the cell as typed, Esc and Alt+Up close it, and any other key closes it and goes on to the grid. `ValidationDropdown` is the arrow right of the active cell, at its bottom, and the list under the cell as wide as the cell and its arrow, over the cell where the view has no room below; `ValidationPrompt` is the input message, a pale yellow box below the cell from its middle, left of that or over the cell where the view ends; `ValidationAlert` is the alert with its style's icon and buttons: Stop has Retry and Cancel, Warning the rule's message, "Continue?" and Yes, No and Cancel, Information OK and Cancel. It opens on Excel's default button (Retry, No, OK) and Esc is Cancel; Yes and OK keep the value, Retry and No open the cell's field again with the text typed, Cancel leaves the cell as it was. Under a finger the arrow and the items are 44 px, and on a phone the alert's buttons too. Their texts are `SheetMessages`' (`validationList`, `validationTitle`, `validationRetry`, `validationYes`, `validationNo`, `validationContinue`, `validationStyle`, with `ok` and `cancel`), their colours `--xs-prompt-*` and `--xs-alert-*` variables. `SheetView` mounts them: the arrow and the message by the active cell when the source has `validation`, the alert when an edit or a Control+Return is refused with `alert`, and an item enters through the source's `pick(sheet, cell, item, text)`, by its place in the list and its text, which writes it as Excel writes a picked item: its value, not read as typed (a list's cell holding the text `001` gives `001`, one showing `50%` the number 0.5), the cell's format kept, the rule not asked. A source without `pick` (`memorySource`) gets the item's text through `edit` with `accept`. While the alert is up the edited cell is the active one and shows the typed text as pending.

## Formula AutoComplete

A source with `functionNames(signal)` gives the functions a formula can call, by their names as the source's locale types them (`СУММ` in ru-RU), in the order the list shows them. While a formula is typed into the cell's field or the formula bar, and the caret stands right after the beginning of a name (letters, digits, `.` and `_` after the formula's `=`, an operator, `(`, an argument separator, a space or a line break; not in a string, a quoted sheet's name or a table's brackets), Excel's list opens under the field: the functions whose names begin with what was typed, in any case, eight at a time with a scroll bar, the first one highlighted. Typing on narrows it, and a character that is no part of a name, or a name no function begins, closes it. The arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End move the highlight; Tab or a double click enters the function in place of the typed name with its `(` (one already after the name is kept) and closes the list; Esc closes the list and keeps the field, and a second Esc cancels the edit as before. Enter commits the cell as typed, as Excel for Windows does; Excel for Mac enters the item on Return too. Left and Right move the caret and the list follows the name before it. Under a finger the items are 44 px and a tap enters one. The sheet asks for the names once per source, when the first field opens, and again for a new source; a request that failed is asked again with the next field, and without `functionNames` there is no list. Excel's tip with a function's arguments is not there: xlcalc has no signatures of its functions yet.

The list is `FunctionList` in the grid's content under the cell's field (over it where the view has no room below), or under the formula bar's field, kept inside the window; its state and keys are `useAutocomplete` (`src/core/autocomplete.ts`), which the sheet's editing makes. Its texts are `SheetMessages`' `functionList` and `functionHint`, its colours `--xs-fl-*` variables. `./local` gives every function xlcalc computes, by its name in the book's locale, English where the locale names none (`ABS`, `SIN` in ru-RU, as Excel shows them), sorted by the locale's alphabet. `memorySource` gives a book's `functions` as they are, and has no `functionNames` without them.

## The sheet's methods

`SheetView` exposes `undo()`, `redo()`, `format(patches)`, `structure(op)`, `outline(change)`, `freezePanes(mode)` and `formatCells()` (opens Format Cells, false when the sheet cannot format) through a template ref, with `reapplyFilter()` (Reapply; false with no display filter in force) and `explain()` (opens the precedents' panel at the active cell; false without the source's `explain`). `freezePanes` takes Excel's command: `'panes'` (the default, at the active cell), `'unfreeze'`, `'topRow'` or `'firstColumn'`, and sends nothing when there is nothing to freeze or unfreeze. Each goes through the source as the sheet's own keys go: the outcome is applied, its selection set without the flash and the step back that `focus` makes, a refusal shown in the sheet's message line, and after `format`, `structure`, `outline` or `freezePanes` the sheet reads itself again unless the source sent `reload`. `format` formats the active sheet: a patch without `range` goes over the selection as Excel writes it (`B:B`, `2:5`). Each returns the source's outcome, or null when nothing was sent (no such method, a read-only sheet, an undo still out).

## The formatting toolbar: `SheetToolbar`

`SheetToolbar` is Excel's formatting row over the same source: undo and redo, bold, italic, underline, font and fill colours from Excel's palette, borders (all, outside, bottom, none), horizontal alignment, wrap text, Excel's Number Format box and, for a host that shows the dialog below, Format Cells. It formats the selection the sheet's `state` event gives, through `format(sheet, patches)`, as Excel formats a selection: bold goes over each cell's look, outside borders are four patches in one call and so one step of undo. Its buttons are pressed by what the active cell shows, and pressed again they take it off, as Excel's are. It shows only when the source has `format`; undo and redo come with the source's `undo` and `redo`, off while its `history` (the local source's) has no step.

```vue
<script setup lang="ts">
import { ref } from 'vue'
import { SheetToolbar, SheetView, ruMessages, type SheetViewApi, type SheetViewState } from 'xlcalc-sheet'

// source: a SheetSource with format, such as localSource(book), whose locale is the book's
const state = ref<SheetViewState | null>(null)
const sheet = ref<SheetViewApi | null>(null)
</script>

<template>
  <SheetToolbar :source="source" :sheet="sheet" :state="state" :messages="ruMessages" locale="ru-RU" />
  <SheetView ref="sheet" :source="source" :messages="ruMessages" @state="state = $event" />
</template>
```

The Number Format box is Excel's, as xlcalc measured it (`numberFormatCategory` and `numberFormatChoices` of `xlcalc/text`, without the engine): it names the category of the active cell's format (General without one, Custom where no category of Excel's holds the code), and its list applies Excel's eleven items (General, Number, Currency, Accounting, Short Date, Long Date, Time, Percentage, Fraction, Scientific, Text), each by the format Excel sets (Percentage is `0.00%`), after which the box names the new category. `locale` is the book's locale, as `SheetView`'s: the names and the Currency item's code are its, ru-RU's loaded as a chunk of its own; en-US without it. With `sheet` (the sheet's template ref) the toolbar formats, undoes and redoes through the sheet's methods (above): the sheet sets the selection and shows a refusal itself. Without it the toolbar calls the source and emits `select` with where the selection goes after an undo or a redo (for the sheet's `focus`) and `error` with a source's refusal. It emits `done` after each call, for the host to give the grid its focus back. Its buttons do not take the focus from the grid. Under 600 px it keeps undo, redo, bold, italic and underline in a row and the rest in a menu; on a narrow frame or under a finger every target is 44 × 44 px.

## Excel's Format Cells dialog: `FormatDialog`

`SheetView` has Excel's Format Cells window of its own when the source has `format`: Control+1 opens it (on a Mac too, as the browser keeps Cmd+1), as do the sheet's `formatCells()` and the toolbar's Format Cells… with `sheet`, and OK formats the selection through the sheet's `format`. The window is also exported as `FormatDialog`, for a host that places its own. It is Excel's Format Cells over the selection the sheet's `state` event gives, with the tabs whose settings xlcalc's cell style holds. Number is Excel's Number Format box: the category of the code in the field, the list's items (an item puts its format's code in the field) and the code itself, which may be any. Alignment has the horizontal and vertical alignments, the indent (with left, right and distributed, as in Excel) and wrapping; Font the font's name, Regular, Italic, Bold or Bold Italic, the size, the underline, strikethrough and the colour; Border a line's kind and colour, the presets None, Outline and Inside and a button for each edge round a preview, the inside ones on a selection of more than one row or column; Fill the background colour. Colours are Excel's palette, as on the toolbar, or any `#RRGGBB`. It opens at the active cell's look, with the edges of one cell as its own sides and a range's as not known, as Excel greys them. OK emits `apply` with patches for `SheetView.format` for what changed alone: the font, the alignment, the fill and the number format over the selection, each border edge on its row, column or the cells on both sides of an inside line (one patch over every cell when all edges take one line), all in one call and so one step of undo; then `close`, as Cancel and Esc do. A field past Excel's limits (a size of 1 to 409 points, an indent of 0 to 250) or a colour code that is none holds OK back. On a phone it takes the whole screen with 44 px targets.

A host with a dialog of its own listens to the toolbar's `formatCells`, which the button then emits instead:

```vue
<SheetToolbar :source="source" :sheet="sheet" :state="state" locale="ru-RU" @format-cells="formatOpen = true" />
<FormatDialog :open="formatOpen" :state="state" :messages="ruMessages" locale="ru-RU" @apply="sheet?.format($event)" @close="formatOpen = false" />
```

Patterns of a fill, diagonal borders, rotated and shrunk text, merging, text direction, superscript, subscript and the Protection tab are not there: xlcalc's cell style has none of them.

## The example: an app like Excel

`example/spa.html` is an app like Excel over the local source, all in the browser: New (an empty book in English or Russian), Open (the picker, or an xlsx file dropped on the page), Save as (the book with its values as `<name>.xlsx`, under the name in the bar), a notice of what the file lost, the toolbar (through the sheet's methods) with Excel's Format Cells dialog, the sheet with its formula bar, tabs, the structure menu on row numbers, column letters and tabs, the cells' menu, the fill handle and selection totals, a status bar (reading the file, computing the book, ready, or calculating while a change is out) and the book's settings (its language, in which formulas, numbers and dates are typed and shown; the book opens again in it, on the sheet it stood on). It speaks Russian or English by the browser's language (`?lang=ru` or `?lang=en` chooses).

A file opens as Excel opens it for its user. xlsx keeps no language, so a file that names none (only xlcalc writes one) opens in the locale of the interface's language, ru-RU under the Russian interface, as Excel shows formulas in its user's language; the settings change it. The app opens the first visible sheet with a value or a formula: Excel opens the sheet active when the file was saved, which xlcalc does not read yet, and a divider sheet at the front would open empty. What the file lost is one line, as Excel's message bar: each kind with its count, what changes values (links to other workbooks, formulas the engine does not parse) first and names last; Details opens each message with its count, the sheets, example cells and names. Reading and writing a file run in a thread of the app (`example/spa-files-worker.ts`), and the book and the values the file stores cross between the threads in slices of 20,000 cells, so a large file does not stop the page while it opens or saves; the book opens with those values, so a circular reference holds what Excel showed.

`npm run dev:example` serves it at `http://localhost:5181/spa.html`. `npm run build:example` builds it into `example/dist`, which any static server serves (`npx vite preview --config vite.example.config.ts`, or `python3 -m http.server -d example/dist`); there is no server of its own. `npm run check:spa` builds it, serves it and drives it in Chromium at 1920 × 1200 and on a phone at 390 × 844 by touch: a ru-RU book, values and a formula, bold, percent, outside borders, undo, redo, Save as, and the saved file opened again; then the structure menu (a row inserted by a right click and by a long press, a column hidden and shown, a sheet added, renamed and deleted, a column fitted by a double click); then the fill handle and cut, copy and paste (a formula dragged three rows down by its handle, a range with a formula copied and pasted, a formula cut and pasted, each undone; Control+C, X and V on the desktop, the cells' menu on the phone); then Formula AutoComplete (`=СУ` in a cell on the desktop and in the formula bar on the phone, СУММ taken from the list by Tab or a tap, the sum checked); then a file with a circular reference opened from the picker, which holds the values it stores through an edit elsewhere; with screenshots of each step in `test-results/screens/spa-*.png`.

`npm run build:demo` builds the app alone for `xlcalc.pridelab.dev/demo/` into `example/dist-demo`: its paths under `/demo/` (the threads too, which load by `import.meta.url`), its `index.html` the app itself, so `/demo/` opens it, and no source maps. Any static server that serves the directory at `/demo/` serves it. `DEMO=1 npm run check:spa` takes the whole check on that build, served under `/demo/`, and checks that `/demo/` is the app and that the build holds no source map.

`npm run check:big` measures the app on large books in Chromium at 1920 × 1200: the production build served by `vite preview` opens each file from its field, and the check measures the first drawn cells of the sheet the app opens and Ready, the jump to the largest sheet, scrolling it down five screens and right three, a number typed into a cell whose dependent is on screen until the dependent shows its new value, Control+Z, Save as (time and size; the saved file is read back and must have the book's sheets and formulas, then it is deleted), the heap of the page and of its Web Workers (CDP), the longest frame gap and long task of each step, and a change of the book's language in its settings. It fails when a file without a language does not open in ru-RU, when the app opens another sheet than the first visible one with data, on a page error or when no edit reaches the screen. Without `BIG_XLSX` it checks itself on xlcalc's synthetic book of 34,000 rows (510,757 cells, written to `test-results`). To run it on your own books, give their paths, joined by `:`; they are read where they lie and nothing is copied:

```sh
BIG_XLSX="$HOME/books/plan.xlsx:$HOME/books/budget.xlsx" npm run check:big
BIG_PROFILE=1 BIG_XLSX=... npm run check:big   # also the page's CPU profiles of each step, test-results/big-*.cpuprofile
```

`npm run check:excel-values` compares the library's values of a book with the values Excel saved in it: each formula cell after a session opened with the file's stored values computes the book (as the app opens a file, so a circular reference holds them) against `readXlsx`'s `cached`, numbers within a relative 1e-9. It prints how many differ and exits 1 when any does (`XLSX=a.xlsx:b.xlsx`; a large book wants `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=12288`).

`npm run check:compare-values` compares the values the app saved in a file with those Excel saved in its copy after a full recalculation (`SAVED=app.xlsx EXCEL=excel.xlsx`): every cell with a value in either file, a value in one only differing, cells of volatile formulas left out; numbers within 1e-12 or a relative 1e-14, dates as serial numbers, text exactly, errors by code. It exits 1 when any differs.

The table goes to the terminal, the numbers to `test-results/big-check.json` and the screenshots to `test-results/screens/big-*.png`. On the synthetic book (Apple M5 Pro, 12.10.2026, the machine busy with other builds) the first sheet is drawn 2.3 to 2.4 s after the file is picked, the dependent shows an edit in 40 to 50 ms and its undo in 25 to 40 ms, Save as takes 4.1 to 4.4 s, and no frame of the page waits longer than 0.3 s.

## A book computed in the browser: `xlcalc-sheet/local`

`localSource(book)` is a `SheetSource` over an xlcalc `BookInput`. It needs `xlcalc` 0.21.1 or later, an optional peer of the package that the host installs. The book computes in a Web Worker (`xlcalc/worker`), or in the page's thread with `calc: 'inline'`. The book's locale module is loaded when the source opens (ru-RU from `xlcalc/locales/ru-RU`); a locale xlcalc has no module for fails the opening with the library's message.

The session does the work as Excel does it, and the source passes it on with every optional method of `SheetSource`. A window is one `getViewport` call: formulas as the book's locale shows them, the text each cell opens with (`editText`), styles, what conditional formats give cells (`appearance`), widths, heights, merged areas and tables. Typed text is read in the book's locale, several cells in one step of the undo (`=СУММ(A1:A2)`, `1 234,5`, `15.03.2026` in ru-RU), and a column a typed number does not fit widens; a number typed over a percent cell takes Excel's `%` (6 is 6%), the cell's own text edited in place (`inPlace`: F2, a double click, the formula bar) does not (6% is 0.06); Delete over a selection clears the content and the hyperlinks, as Excel's Delete. `paste` takes clipboard text by Excel's rules for a paste (an apostrophe stays in the text, a percent cell has no percent editor), `fill` is Control+Return, `undo` and `redo` are the session's, whole. `validation` gives the session's rule over a cell (`getValidation`): a list's items as the cells show them, by their number format, when the rule shows its dropdown, and the input message unless the rule turns it off. `pick` writes an item with the session's `setCells` as Excel writes it (xlcalc 0.29 measured it in Excel for Mac): a list of cells gives the cell's value as it is, a literal list's item is read as typed in en-US in any book (`"001,1/2,x"` gives 1 and the 2nd of January in `d-mmm`); the list is read again first, and an item whose text is no longer at its place is refused (`conflict`). A value a rule of data validation of any style does not take is refused with the rule's alert before anything is written, and the history stays as it was; several cells at once are refused by the strictest rule they break, as xlcalc's `refuseInvalid` picks it, and Control+Return by the active cell's rule. With `accept` the texts are written without the check, as Excel writes them after Yes or OK; a rule with `showError: false` takes any value, as Excel's does. Structure operations, widths and heights, formatting over each cell's look (`format`, as Excel's Bold over a selection), frozen panes (`freeze`, one `setLayout` call and one step of the undo), `explain`, totals, Find and Replace, the display filter and sort complete it. Find looks in the text each cell opens with, read once per sheet in windows of 20,000 cells and kept with the edits (1.4 s the first time on the synthetic book of 34,000 rows, some 35 ms after), or in the shown text; Replace All writes the hits' new texts in one `setCellTexts`, as typed into each cell, without widening a column or checking data validation. A sheet's `outline` is xlcalc's `outlineGroups`: a group of level L is a longest run of lines at L or deeper, collapsed when Excel keeps the mark on its summary line, which it sets as soon as the lines under it are all hidden, by hand too, and keeps on a summary line hidden inside a hidden group. `outline(change)` is one of xlcalc's structure operations (`editStructure`), one step of the undo with SUBTOTAL and AGGREGATE recomputed, by the rules xlcalc measured in Excel for Windows (its `docs/outline.md`): Group puts each line a level deeper, those at the seventh staying there, and is refused when all of them are; Ungroup takes a level off the lines that have one and leaves hidden lines hidden; a group's − and + are Hide and Show Detail at its summary line, which act on that line's whole detail, so the button of an inner group that shares its summary line with the outer one hides and shows both; Show Detail shows the lines hidden by hand too, all but those of the groups inside whose summary line keeps its mark; a level button hides the lines of that level and deeper, shows the other grouped lines, those hidden by hand among them, and leaves lines outside groups as they are. A refusal is the library's, in the book's language. A group at the sheet's edge (rows 1:3 with summaries above) has no summary line and, as in Excel, no button: the grid draws none, and a command for it is refused. `memorySource` keeps the component's earlier rules (`src/memory-outline.ts`), which miss the shared summary line and the marks set by hand and kept under a level button. `openXlsx` reads a file into a book and the values it stores (`cached`), and `saveXlsx` writes the source's book with its values; with `locale` (the language the person chose: a tag, its module loaded on demand, or a locale object) their issues' messages are in that language, in English without it; a tag xlcalc has no module for fails with the library's message.

```ts
import { SheetView } from 'xlcalc-sheet'
import { localSource, openXlsx, saveXlsx } from 'xlcalc-sheet/local'

const { book, cached, issues } = await openXlsx(file, { locale: 'ru-RU' }) // a File from an input or a drop; issues: what the book cannot hold
const source = localSource(book, { session: { cached } })                   // <SheetView :source="source" :locale="source.locale" />
const { data } = await saveXlsx(source)                                     // the xlsx bytes, formulas with their values
```

A book read from a file opens as Excel opens the file with the values it stores, given to the session as `session: { cached }` (xlcalc's `SessionOptions.cached`, 0.27 or later): a circular reference without iterative calculation is not computed, and its cells and those that read them hold the values the file stores, through edits elsewhere, until a value typed into the cycle breaks it (xlcalc's `docs/cycles.md`). Without them those cells hold 0. The session's book keeps what they hold (`held`), so a book taken with `book()` opens holding it again, and `saveXlsx` stores it.

The pool starts its thread as `new Worker(new URL('./thread.js', import.meta.url))`, which Vite and other bundlers follow; where a bundler serves the thread elsewhere, pass `pool: { spawn }`. Besides `SheetSource` the source has `ready`, `locale`, `history` (the steps `undo` and `redo` can take, for a toolbar), `book()`, `snapshot()` (the book and its values taken at one moment, what `writeXlsx` takes; it replaces `result()` of 0.2) and `close()`. It tells its subscribers of every change it makes: `changed` for edits and widths, `reload` for structure, styles, lines, frozen panes and their undo. An edit, a paste, a fill, an undo or a redo in a book with conditional formats sends `repaint` after its `changed`, with the ranges of the book's rules the session gives: their cells may look otherwise anywhere the rules reach (a colour scale recolours as one number changes), and a cell update does not carry a look. The values the source keeps for search and the filter stay then, unless a rule shows numbers by a format of its own, and a display filter keeps its rows. `npm run check:local` opens the example's `local.html` (the synthetic book of 3,400 rows) in Chromium, measures it and takes screenshots; the page also opens an xlsx file from its field and saves the book under the file's name.
