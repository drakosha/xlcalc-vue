# Reapply, Freeze Panes and the precedents

Under a display filter the cells' menu has Excel's Reapply, as do Control+Alt+L (on a Mac too, where Cmd+Option+L is the browser's) and a button on the filter's line at the bottom: the conditions and the sort are applied again to the data as they are now, through `visibleRows`, so a row an edit took out of them goes and one it brought in comes. Until then such a row stays, as in Excel. The scroll stays, and so does the active cell unless its row went, when the row now at its place takes it.

With the source's `freeze(sheet, panes)` on an editable sheet the menu has Excel's Freeze Panes, Freeze Top Row and Freeze First Column, and on a sheet the book freezes Unfreeze Panes takes the first one's place. Freeze Panes keeps the rows above and the columns left of the active cell in view, so a selection of whole columns freezes the columns left of it and one of whole rows the rows above it; from A1 it freezes the rows and columns above and left of the frame's middle, as Excel splits the window there. Each is the source's step of undo, and the sheet reads itself again after it unless the source sent `reload`. The lock on the formula bar stays a view over the book's panes: it hides them or shows them again in this browser and leaves the book alone. A freeze made from the menu shows whatever the lock said before and however much of the frame the panes take, and an unfreeze forgets the lock's choice. Without `freeze` there are no such items, and the lock works as before; under the display filter there are none either, as its rows on screen are not the sheet's.

With the source's `explain(sheet, cell)` the menu has Trace Precedents. Where Excel draws arrows, the sheet opens a panel right of the grid (below it on a phone) with the source's tree of the active cell's precedents. The panel follows the active cell while it is open: it asks again 150 ms after the cell stops moving or its value changes, and when the source tells of changes; an answer that comes after a newer request is dropped, as `explain` takes no signal. A line is a node and two spaces of indent are a level, as xlcalc's `explain` writes its text (`'Q 4'!A2 = 60  ← Data!A4*2`). The references in it to cells, columns or rows of one sheet are links, a reference without a sheet is on its line's node's sheet, and a click goes there as Ctrl+click on a reference in the formula bar does; the panel then explains the cell it went to. The cross and Esc close it. `./local` gives the session's `explain` in the book's locale, and `memorySource` writes a tree from the formulas as given, in xlcalc's English words. The panel's width, and its height on a phone, are `--xs-explain-w` (340 px) and `--xs-explain-h` (45 %).
