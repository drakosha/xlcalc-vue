# Slicers and timelines

A workbook's slicers and timelines over PivotTables lie over the cells at their places, as Excel draws them, and filter their PivotTables as Excel's do: a click on a slicer's item, Ctrl+click, Multi-Select, Clear Filter, a run of a timeline's periods. The sheet sends each change to the source's `structure` as one of xlcalc's operations (`editSlicer`, `editTimeline`, `addSlicer`, `addTimeline`, `deleteSlicer`, `deleteTimeline`), one step of undo, and reads the sheets again afterwards, so the PivotTables they filter show the new choice and the slicers their items with data anew. A refresh and an undo reload them too.

## What the source gives

A source lists each sheet's slicers in `SheetInfo.slicers` and its timelines in `SheetInfo.timelines`, read with the sheets ([api.md](api.md#windows-and-sheets)). Each is xlcalc's `Slicer` or `Timeline` with what its cache holds:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | Unique among the workbook's slicers and timelines: an operation names it. |
| `caption`, `showCaption` (`showHeader` of a timeline) | The header's text, and whether the header shows. |
| `place` | xlcalc's `DrawingPlace`: the cells of its top-left and bottom-right corners (`from`, `to`) with offsets into them in EMU (914,400 to an inch, 9,525 to a pixel). A slicer or timeline without one is not drawn. |
| `field`, `pivots`, `pivotCache` | The source field it filters, the PivotTables it filters (`{ sheet, name }`), their pivot cache's id. |
| `items` (a slicer) | Its items in the slicer's order (`SheetSlicerItem`): `item`, the index `editSlicer` names it by; `label`, as the slicer shows it; `selected: false` for an item not chosen; `noData: true` for an item no record of which passes the other filters. |
| `columns`, `rowHeight`, `sort`, `customListSort`, `noData`, `showMissing` (a slicer) | Its columns of buttons, their height in EMU, and its cache's sorting and display of items. |
| `level`, `selectionLevel`, `selection`, `bounds` (a timeline) | The level shown (months by default), the level the dates were chosen at, the dates chosen (`{ start, end }`, none for all periods), the dates it spans (whole years). |

`@xlcalc/vue/local` gives them from the workbook's layout, where xlcalc keeps each slicer cache's items with whether each is chosen and has data after every calculation. An item's label is xlcalc's (`getLayout().slicerCaches[].items[].label`, 0.72): the text Excel shows on its button in the workbook's locale, the value in the field's number format on its first PivotTable, a date as the short date, a time alone as its time, a blank `(blank)` (`(пусто)` in ru-RU). `SheetInfo.pivots` gives each PivotTable's pivot cache (`cache`) and the cache's fields (`fields`, `{ name, dates? }`, without calculated fields) for Insert Slicer and Report Connections.

## How they are drawn

Each slicer and timeline is a box in the grid's scroller, at its place, so it scrolls and zooms with the cells. With frozen panes each pane draws its part of the box, as Excel draws an object in each pane: the part in the frozen rows or columns stays while the rest scrolls under them. Each pane of a split window draws its own. The grid reaches as far as the sheet's slicers and timelines, a read-only sheet's too, as Excel scrolls to them, so a sheet of objects alone shows them; a place beyond the rows and columns drawn is measured by their sizes. An offset larger than its cell stops at the cell's edge, as an anchor's offset lies within its cell (ECMA-376's `colOff` and `rowOff`; what Excel does with a larger one was not measured), so an anchor in a hidden column lies at its edge. Under the display filter a box keeps the height of its sheet rows from its top-left row, those the filter leaves out counting as hidden; a box whose top-left row the filter leaves out is not drawn.

The look is a plain neutral one, the same for every style: the workbook's `style` (`SlicerStyleLight1`, `TimeSlicerStyleLight1` and the others) is kept for Excel and not drawn yet. The CSS variables `--xs-slicer-*` set its colors ([api.md](api.md#css-variables)).

- A slicer: a frame; the header with its caption, Multi-Select and Clear Filter; a button for each item in `columns` columns, `rowHeight` high (Excel's 0.26 inch without one). A chosen item is filled, an item not chosen is plain, an item with no data has grey text and a dashed border (where the cache marks them), and the items scroll when they do not fit. With Hide items with no data, those items are not shown.
- A timeline: the header with its caption and Clear Filter; the selection's label (All Periods, `Mar 2025`, `Jan - Jun 2025`) and the level picker (YEARS, QUARTERS, MONTHS, DAYS); the periods of the level between its bounds under their years (under their months for days), the chosen ones filled, scrolled sideways, opening at the first chosen period.

## Choosing

- A click on a slicer's item chooses it alone. Ctrl+click adds it or takes it away; so does a click while Multi-Select is on (the header's button, Alt+S, or the menu's Multi-Select). Shift+click chooses the run of items from the last one clicked, added to the choice with Ctrl or Multi-Select. Taking away the last item chosen clears the filter (not measured in Excel). Excel does not save Multi-Select in the file, and the sheet keeps it while the page lives.
- Clear Filter (the header's button, Alt+C, or the menu's Clear Filter from "X") chooses every item, or takes a timeline's dates away (`selection: null`).
- A press on a timeline's period chooses it, a drag over periods chooses the run, Shift+click extends the run from the last period pressed, or without one from the dates chosen before (a period is kept by its first day, so it holds after a change of level); the dates sent are the first day of the first period to the last day of the last, which xlcalc applies by whole days.
- The level picker changes the level shown (`editTimeline` `set.level`), which does not change the dates; a press outside its list or Esc closes the list.

Each change is one step of undo, as xlcalc makes it. Without xlcalc's PivotTable engine the choice is written all the same and the PivotTables keep their cells until a refresh ([pivot-tables.md](pivot-tables.md#operations)).

## Selecting, moving, resizing, removing

A press on a slicer or a timeline selects it, as Excel's does: `state.object` names it, its frame takes the keys and shows its handles. A drag of the frame (its header, its edge) moves it, a drag of a handle resizes it; the release sends its new place (`set.place`, the corners' cells and offsets), as one step of undo. Delete removes it (`deleteSlicer`, `deleteTimeline`); Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo as on the grid; Esc gives the keys back to the grid; a cell made active by any means (a press, the Name Box, Go To, the keys) leaves it. A drag follows the pointer over the cells under it, so a box dragged from frozen rows into the scrolled ones stays under the pointer. A slicer with Disable resizing and moving on (xlcalc's `Slicer.positionLocked`) is not moved or resized on any sheet: it has no handles, and the local source refuses a new place for it. While the display filter shows rows in another order than the sheet's, nothing is moved or resized, since a place in the rows on screen would not be one in the sheet.

A right click opens its menu with the items of Excel for Windows' menu of a slicer the sheet has, in Excel's order and groups (measured in Excel 16.0): Refresh (the PivotTables it filters); Sort A to Z and Sort Z to A; Clear Filter from "X", Multi-Select "X", Report Connections…, Remove "X"; Slicer Settings…. Excel's Cut, Copy, Paste Options, Group, Bring to Front, Send to Back, Assign Macro…, View Alt Text… and Size and Properties… are not there. A timeline's menu has those of them a timeline can have, in the same order (not measured). Remove and Multi-Select name the slicer by its name (Microsoft's "Remove <Name of Slicer>"), Clear Filter from by its caption.

## Insert Slicer and Insert Timeline

`SheetViewApi.insertSlicer()` opens Excel's Insert Slicers dialog over the PivotTable under the active cell, a box for each field of its cache; OK adds a slicer of each field checked (`addSlicer` with the field and that PivotTable) and selects the last. `insertTimeline()` opens Insert Timelines with the fields of dates, or, without one, Excel's alert "We can't create a Timeline for this report because it doesn't have a field formatted as Date."; OK adds a timeline of each field checked. `state.canInsertSlicer` says they are available. xlcalc names a new slicer after its field (`Region`, `Region 1`). The sheet sends its place, which only the sheet knows: Excel puts a new slicer or timeline at the middle of the window, and the sheet puts it at the middle of the cells the active pane shows, in Excel's size (a slicer 144 × 198.75 pt, a timeline 262.5 × 108 pt, as the library measured Excel), each next one of one insert 20 px lower and to the right, as Excel's Insert Slicers cascades them (the step was not measured). The last one inserted is selected and its frame takes the keys, so Delete removes it; an insert the source refuses selects the last one added before it, or none.

## Slicer Settings and Report Connections

`slicerSettings()` (and the menu's Slicer Settings…) opens Excel's dialog for the selected slicer: Source Name and Name to use in formulas (the field and the slicer cache's name), Name (a new one renames the slicer, which stays selected, through `editSlicer`'s `set.name` of xlcalc 0.72; OK waits while the box is empty or blank, so the dialog stays with what it holds and no empty name is sent, as Excel takes no slicer without a name (its own alert there was not measured); one another slicer or timeline has in any case is xlcalc's refusal, shown as any refusal), Header with Display header and Caption, and Item Sorting and Filtering with Ascending (A to Z), Descending (Z to A), Use Custom Lists when sorting, Hide items with no data, Visually indicate items with no data, Show items with no data last and Show items deleted from the data source. OK sends them as one `editSlicer` (`set.caption`, `set.showCaption`, `cache.sort`, `cache.customListSort`, `cache.noData`, `cache.showMissing`). Excel's variants of the sort options for numbers and dates (Smallest to Largest, Oldest to Newest) are not drawn.

`reportConnections()` (and the menu's Report Connections…) opens Excel's Report Connections for the selected slicer or timeline: every PivotTable of the workbook over the same pivot cache, by name and sheet, those it filters checked. OK sends the PivotTables checked as `cache.pivots`; a PivotTable connected takes the slicer's choice, one disconnected shows every item again, as xlcalc does.

## Protection

Microsoft's Protect a worksheet says slicers and timelines are locked when added, that the lock works once the sheet is protected, and that Use PivotTable reports lets users "format, change the layout, refresh, or otherwise modify PivotTable reports"; its object model says a slicer whose `Locked` is false "can be modified when the sheet is protected". `@xlcalc/vue/local` checks a protected sheet ([pivot-tables.md](pivot-tables.md#protection)): a choice, a level, a place, settings and Report Connections need Use PivotTable and PivotChart on the sheets of the PivotTables the cache filters; adding and removing need Use PivotTable and PivotChart on its sheet too; adding, and moving, resizing and removing a locked one, need Edit objects on its sheet. A slicer or a timeline is locked unless its Locked is off (xlcalc 0.72's `Slicer.locked` and `Timeline.locked`, true by default as in Excel). The rule is one function both use (`objectNeeds` of `src/protection.ts`): the sheet disables what the source refuses, by the protection of every sheet it names. None of this was measured in Excel; the alert is the protected sheet's.

## Texts

The texts are `SlicerMessages` of `SheetMessages`, in English and Russian, as Excel for Windows 16.0 shows them (measured in en-US and ru-RU): Insert Slicers and Insert Timelines (OK unavailable until a field is checked), the slicer's menu, Slicer Settings, the levels of a timeline, All Periods, Clear Filter (Alt+C) of the slicer and the timeline, and the selection's label (`Mar - May 2026`, `мар — май 2026 г.`). Three come from elsewhere: the Multi-Select tooltip (Multi-Select (Alt+S), Выбор нескольких объектов (ALT+S)) and the English alert over a PivotTable without dates are Excel's string tables (the Russian alert was seen in Excel for Windows), and the labels of one day, of years and of periods across years are their patterns; the short names of the months are the locale's and show in capitals on the periods, as Excel draws them.
